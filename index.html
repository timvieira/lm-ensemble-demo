    What this shows

    Each dot is a completion, placed by how likely it is after prompt A (across) and after prompt B (up). The completions were drawn from the token-level product of the two prompts: at every step, the next token is picked in proportion to pA·pB.

    τ chooses how the two probabilities are combined over the whole string: the generalised mean (Σ ½ pkτ)1/τ. At the left end, the minimum: a completion is only as good as its worse prompt says (agreement). In the middle, the product. Then the sum, a mixture, and at the right end the maximum: either prompt liking it is enough (coverage). The grey lines are where that combined score is constant; the list on the right ranks the completions by it, and the red points are the best five.

    Scored: local uses the token-level product's own string probability instead, the usual way of ensembling. It favours openings both prompts like one token at a time, rather than completions both like as a whole (the paper's Fig. 1).

    Each completion's probability under the ensemble is its score divided by the ensemble's normaliser, estimated from the N completions with importance weights (the score divided by the probability of drawing the completion). "Uses n of N" is the effective sample size of those weights: how many completions that estimate really rests on.
